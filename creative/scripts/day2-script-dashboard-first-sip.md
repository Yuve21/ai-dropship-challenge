## The five Dashboard-and-Pointer elements, adapted for a physical product
1. The product doing its trick, one continuous unedited shot.
2. A real finger pointing at the exact moment it works.
3. One bright 1-3 word overlay naming that moment.
4. Original audio, no trending sound, no voiceover.
5. Caption under six words, plus 5-10 hashtags.

## Hook (first 3 seconds, the opening VISUAL frame)
Camera is already rolling, framed on the running fountain (water audibly moving) with the cat mid-approach,
already inside the frame, closing the last foot of distance to the water. The hook is not "a cat walks up,"
it is the cat already almost there when the clip starts, because a day-one account gets no benefit from a
slow build. First frame shows motion and intent.

## Shot list, one unbroken take
| Time | What happens | Camera |
|---|---|---|
| 0-2s | Cat closes the last distance to the fountain, water already moving. | Locked medium-close angle, phone steady, no reframing. |
| 2-4s | Cat's tongue or mouth makes contact with the moving water stream for the first time in this clip. | Same frame, hold. |
| 4-6s | A finger enters frame from the side and points at the exact spot of contact, held for a beat. | Same frame. |
| 6-9s | Bright overlay text appears at the point of contact, timed to the finger. | Same frame, overlay only. |
| 9-11s | Cat keeps drinking, finger stays in frame a moment longer, then the shot ends. No cut to a second angle. | Same frame throughout. |

**Filming note:** the "wait for it" nature of this shot means multiple real takes may be filmed off-camera
patience-permitting; only the one unbroken successful take gets posted. No trimming or re-ordering inside
that clip.

The pinned comment is a two-option poll ("tap" vs "ignores the bowl") disguised as a genuine question about
the viewer's own cat, not about this product. Low friction (one word to type), and it works whether or not
the viewer has this product, which widens who can plausibly answer.

instant of first contact with moving water) that currently reads as fake the moment it's generated, and a
fake-looking version of the single most important frame in the video defeats the whole script.
